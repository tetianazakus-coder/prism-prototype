What is clickable, where every value comes from, and what we invented
  • Tokens are the repo's, copied not matched. apps/lender-prism/frontend/src/app/globals.css — --primary navy-900 rgb(1,39,88), --sidebar navy-950 rgb(8,20,45), --radius 0.5rem, the eight-step shadow ramp, --tracking-normal -0.011em.
  • Component sizes are the repo's too. Button h-9 / h-8 / h-6, rounded-md; Badge rounded-full px-2 py-0.5 text-xs; Card rounded-xl border shadow-sm; Table th h-10 px-2 font-medium; Input h-9; Checkbox size-4 rounded-[4px]; sidebar 3rem collapsed, menu buttons h-8 rounded-md p-2. One departure: the rail is drawn at 14rem, not the shipped 16rem — the five nav labels are short and the widest thing in it is the org line, so the extra 32px was empty. Changing SIDEBAR_WIDTH in ui/sidebar.tsx is a one-line edit. [💡 rec]
  • The responsive layer is ours; only the breakpoint is the repo's. There was nothing to copy — app/(dashboard)/my-deals/page.tsx carries two responsive utilities in 385 lines (sm:px-6, lg:px-8) over a plain desktop table, so below about 1100px the columns squeeze until deal names wrap one word per line. What the repo does settle is where mobile starts: hooks/use-mobile.ts MOBILE_BREAKPOINT = 768, and ui/sidebar.tsx swaps the rail for a Sheet at SIDEBAR_WIDTH_MOBILE = 18rem below it. Both are honoured — 767 is the phone edge and the rail becomes an 18rem overlay with a scrim. Above it, two steps of our own: at 1180 the fixed 1180 slab is released and tables scroll inside their card rather than squeeze; at 900 the page header, toolbar and two-column deal summary stack. Below 767 each table row becomes a labelled block — the column headings survive as the labels — and a board lane fills the screen. [💡 rec] [repo Aug 26]
  • The sidebar is not a proposal — it already ships. components/app-nav-sidebar.tsx renders a collapsible rail for brokers today. What we changed is which items are in it: Lender Funnel is retired [agreed 14 Aug] and Deals takes its place. ⚠ The shipped rail already has an item called My Deals and it is a different object — the assigned-leads pipeline off /api/assigned_leads. That collision needs settling with the deal-object naming.
  • Active nav tint is --sidebar-accent (navy-700), which is what SidebarMenuButton actually binds — not --sidebar-border. Collapse the rail with the control beside the wordmark.
  • One account menu, not two profile entries. The shipped app splits “you” across two surfaces: a Broker Profile nav item (/broker-profile, pro brokers only — the public directory listing: headshot, cover photo, submit for admin approval) and the top-bar dropdown (components/global/top-nav.tsx: Profile Settings → /profile, Sign out). Same word, two objects, two places. [💡 rec] consolidated here: the account button at the foot of the rail opens one menu — Public profile · Profile Settings · Sign out — and the nav item is gone. The routes stay separate; only the entry point merges.
  • The filters are the repo's own MultiSelect — ui/multi-select.tsx, a Popover over a Command list, selected values rendered as Badge variant="outline" inside the trigger with a +N overflow badge and a CheckIcon per row. One deviation: the field's name stays on the trigger, because the repo puts it in a Label above the field and a filter toolbar has nowhere to put one. Counts beside each option are live and computed against the other filter, so a zero means that combination is genuinely empty.
  • Property type is the canonical twenty-four, not the eight on this page [repo Aug 14] [T · Figma comment, 21 Aug] — the filter is pinned to PROPERTY_TYPES in packages/constants/index.ts, so it offers every type the product stores. A row dims only when your other filters empty it — a type no sample deal uses stays regular, because over a canonical vocabulary absence is the normal state, not a signal. Tony’s comment asks the filter to “match the property types we have for universal searches and lender criteria in all submission forms”; two things still disagree with it and neither is ours to settle. His own Zoho submission form carries 22 of these — no Build to Sell Single-Family & Condos, no Note Purchase Financing — and the shipped filter rail on My Deals and Lenders offers four coarse buckets (Multifamily · Commercial · Industrial · Retail) over rows that display the granular values. ⚠ Which list is the master is question 75. The eight group headings here are the rollup that lets one list serve both shapes — browse coarse, store granular — and they are [💡 rec] ours: they are not the seven licensable asset classes, which cover only eighteen of the twenty-four. The picker itself follows Janover Pro’s own property-type control [comp 28 Aug]: an accordion — group rows hold their place and expand beneath themselves with a child count and rotating chevron, several open at once, checkboxes on the granular values. Two departures from Janover, both Tanya’s (28 Aug): the group row carries its own checkbox — check to take the whole category, a dash marks a partial selection — and a one-value group never renders as a group, its value stands at the top level instead (Hotel/Motel, Land), because expanding a row to reveal one row is pure indirection. It replaced a drill (panel-swap behind a back row), which hid the other groups while you browsed one.
  • Transaction type came off the filter bar, 26 Aug [💡 rec] — it briefly sat there pinned to the three values Tony’s Zoho forms fix (Acquisition · Refinance · Construction loan, per his Figma comment #2), then every line of evidence pointed the other way: the matching query never reads transaction_type, six of the eight deal names already carry the word, the shipped My Deals rows don’t print it, and Janover’s board neither shows nor filters it — transaction words live in its free-text deal titles. The vocabulary fix his comment asked for survives where the field still shows: the deal detail’s Transaction fact says Acquisition, not Purchase. Type of Financing — the facet his comment’s three surfaces actually share — holds the filter slot instead. ⚠ Whether transaction type is a concept of its own at all is question 76.
  • Type of Financing joins the filter bar, the table and the cards [💡 rec · comp 26 Aug] — three independent sources elevate the same facet: the matching query scores on type_of_financing and never reads transaction_type; the shipped My Deals rows print it beside city and state; and Janover Pro’s deals board filters by it under the name Execution Types (its bar: Search · Property Types · Execution Types · Footprints), leaving transaction words — “Land Acquisition”, “Leveraged Buyout”, “Sale Leaseback” — to free-text deal titles. The filter carries the canonical 17 with the same type-ahead as Property type (17 also crosses the scan-vs-type threshold); the table adds a Type of Financing column and a sortable Updated column — recency is the one signal the table lacked, and both the live rows and Janover show it; cards get the financing value under the property line. Sample deals carry one financing value each — except Gulf Coast, which carries none, because the shipped request left the optional field blank; the table prints Not specified and the board card drops the fact rather than showing an empty row. The real column is an array.
  • The facet is labeled Execution Type, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — every surface that showed Financing or Type of Financing (filter, table column, board card, deal summary fact, form label) now says Execution type. Tanya’s call, and the vocabulary was already converging there: Janover Pro’s board filters it as Execution Types, Tony’s Zoho forms ask Executions to Explore [T], and the live form buries the same dropdown under a Transaction Details heading it shares with the Purchase/Refinance radio — a heading that names neither field. The data key stays type_of_financing; the label is the design decision.
  • Branch fields under the Transaction segment, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — picking a segment now reveals the branch the live form and Tony’s Zoho §4 both keep: Acquisition — purchase price, under-contract status, target closing date; Refinance — property value, outstanding loan balance; Construction — total project cost, shovel-ready [T]. Two divergences are deliberate: the live form hard-requires Under Contract and Outstanding Balance — here they are soft nudges in the warn panel, keeping creation under a minute; and Construction has fields at all — the live form has no Construction branch. Only the visible branch saves; switching segments discards the other branch’s values, as the live form’s hidden fields do.
  • Overview coaching moved from tooltip to rail, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — the live form hides its best guidance behind a “Tips for a good summary” hover: cash-out purpose, rehab scope/costs/status, refi history, purchase cap-rate and submarket. The same prose now sits in the “Make this deal stronger” rail, keyed to the selected Transaction segment and visible without hovering — Acquisition and Refinance lines carry the tooltip’s wording; the Construction line (budget, timeline, lease-up plan) is composed from Tony’s Zoho construction checklist [T], since the live form has no Construction branch to coach. The tip shows while the overview is empty, then yields the rail to the next gap.
  • Upload dropzone joins the Documents card, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — both intakes have one (the live form’s “Supporting Documents” takes PDF/Word/Excel/images, 10 MB, 10 files max; Janover’s Documents card takes 50 MB multiples), so the prototype now shows the pattern: choose-files button, drag and drop, a removable file list. Simulated honestly — only names are recorded, the files are never read. The checklist chips came off the form the same day — next to a real picker, mark-without-file read as a duplicate upload control (Tanya, on review). The checklist question — what lenders expect vs what the broker has — moves to the deal page, where the packaging phase belongs; the form’s soft nudge now simply counts uploads.
  • Street address and opt-in data points, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — the location card gains an optional street address (Zoho asks Property Address* [T], Janover requires it, the live form never asks); the map stays off the form — even Janover draws it on the OM editor, not at creation. Below location, a Property details card shows Units, Square footage, Year built and Occupancy as four plain fields — Tanya tried and rejected two opt-in variants (chips, then Janover’s add-zone) before settling on always-visible. Units and Year built are hard-required, matching Tony’s Zoho form where Number of Units | NRSF and Year Built carry the asterisk [T]; the units requirement accepts square footage instead, preserving the Zoho pair for CRE deals. The deal page already rendered units, occupancy and year built from seed data — created deals now feed the same facts. A rail tip carries Janover’s nudge verbatim in spirit: data points strengthen the deal.
  • Highlights and Photos land on the deal page, 28 Aug [💡 rec] — Phase 3 opens where Janover keeps both: the packaging surface, not the create form. Highlights sit above the overview prose — Janover’s own copy calls them “the first thing lenders read” — as an editable 3–5 bullet list with an inert Generate with AI affordance; Riverside ships with three sample bullets. Photos take the right rail above the map schematic: hero image and thumbnail strip. Both sections read clean and edit behind a per-section toggle — a first version put inputs, remove buttons and the AI affordance straight into the view, and Tanya called it “too much info and actions”; LinkedIn’s profile (pencil per section), Julienne’s recipe editor and Wix’s per-block dialogs all draw the same line, and Janover’s own cards carry per-section Edit buttons. Now ✎ Edit reveals the highlight input, per-bullet remove and the AI button (or per-photo remove, the + tile and Janover’s 3+-photos nudge), Done puts the section back to read; edit state resets on navigation. Chosen images render as real client-side thumbnails via object URLs — displayed, never uploaded — which is one step more honest than the document dropzone, because a photo’s whole value is visible. Reworked 28 Aug late evening [💡 rec] to the listing-manager convention Turo, Etsy, Zillow and Airbnb all share: a tile grid with drag-to-reorder, the first tile badged Cover (it feeds the deal page’s OM preview card), the add tile inside the grid, and image files droppable straight onto it. The schematic’s caption loses its “neither is collected today” claim: photos now are.
  • Option F: the OM preview card, 28 Aug evening [💡 rec] — Tanya’s screenshots of Janover’s live deal detail settled the layout question: their deal page is a lender-communications workspace, and the package appears only as a compact preview — one photo thumbnail beside the overview text. Six layouts were mocked (assets/photos-layout-options.html); Tanya picked F, Janover’s own answer. The deal summary now shows the overview as that card — cover image left (168×112, first photo; an N photos badge overlays when there are more — the Airbnb/Zillow card convention; clicking opens a lightbox — arrows, counter, Esc/backdrop close) when a photo exists, plain prose when not, zero layout jump — the right rail’s photo hero/strip is gone, and the map shows just the street address the form collects beneath it — a Location caption and a “schematic” disclaimer were tried and cut (Tanya, same evening): the map is self-labeling, and the address line says everything. Photo management stays in the edit form. Highlights came off the page the same evening (Tanya): Janover’s deal page carries no highlights either — bullets live inside the OM editor, and after F the preview card already tells the story; who writes highlights stays an open client question, so the section waits for a real package surface.
  • Form-to-summary correspondence audit, 28 Aug evening [💡 rec] — Tanya asked whether every create/edit section reaches the Deal summary; six breaks found and closed. Square footage, target closing date, shovel-ready and refinance property value were collected but never displayed — now in the size and Transaction facts. Leverage was the reverse, displayed from seeds but never collected — now computed from loan ÷ purchase price (or property value on a refinance) when no seed value exists. Worst: uploaded documents were invisible — the chips and the Documents tab read only the four-item intake checklist, so a deal created with three real uploads claimed “Nothing was attached”; uploads now lead the Documents tab and every document count includes them. The chips row itself then came off the summary (Tanya, minutes later) — it duplicated the tab, and a row of “· none” chips is noise; documents live in the tab alone. One logic bug rode along: the units fact was gated on occupancy, so 42 units with occupancy blank rendered nothing.
  • Location filter became State, 27 Aug [💡 rec] — the old facet’s values were literal “City, ST” strings scraped off the sample rows, one per deal. Three reasons state is the right grain: the matching query scores geography by state and never reads city; Tony’s intake form asks State* with MSA cut and postal code separate; and the create form leaves city optional, so a city-less deal rendered a “, TX” badge. The facet now carries the canonical 51 with the same type-ahead as Property type. The table’s Location column briefly kept city + state; on 28 Aug it became State too, printing the full state name — the shipped My Deals row does exactly this (State “Florida”, City blank on most rows), and city + postal code are optional, so the list never shows fields a deal may not have. The deal detail keeps the full address — the record view is where optional fields belong.
  • The board follows Janover Pro’s Current Deals board [comp Aug 19] — flat grey lanes on a horizontal scroller, a tone dot and count pill in each lane head, a dashed “No deals” placeholder, card anatomy of title › amount › property type › footer (document and saved-lender counts, date), and a kebab menu on the card with Change Status as a submenu — Janover’s path for the same edit. Every count on the footer is real: documents from the deal’s checklist, saved lenders from the shortlist, the date from updated. Tokens, radii and control metrics stay LenderPrism’s. One footer element did not survive the copy: Janover’s owner chip (avatar + “You”) came off, 26 Aug — loan_requests has a single belongs_to :user, no assignee or team concept, and a broker sees only their own deals, so the chip would read “You” on every card and carry no information. If team seats ever arrive, it returns as the highest-value element on the card. [💡 rec]
  • Both views are held to one height. [💡 rec] Toggling list ↔ board changed the card's height by roughly 300px, which reads as a page reload rather than a change of representation — the control swaps how the same eleven deals are drawn, so nothing below it should move. Both are now pinned to --view-h, measured off the table's own body at eleven rows: the board fills it and each lane scrolls inside itself, the table scrolls under a stuck header if the set ever grows past eleven. Lane heads stay put and the cards move under them, which is what every board holding a real pipeline does. Released below 767, where a table row is a labelled block and no one constant can hold both shapes.
  • Three deals were added on 26 Aug for the board's shape, not for volume. [💡 rec] Eleven lanes each holding exactly one card is the one distribution a real pipeline never has — work piles up at the front and thins toward the close. Harborview, Magnolia and Foundry (the three already drawn in the Figma Deals table frame) sit at Leads and Screening, giving 3 · 3 · 1 · 2 · 1 · 0 · 1 · 0 across the eight statuses. Nothing about them is hand-authored beyond the deal record: their match counts (2 · 1 · 4) fall out of the same five-criteria scoring every other deal runs through, and their saved-lender counts are zero because nothing has been shortlisted at those stages. Funded and On Hold stay empty, so the dashed “No deals” placeholder is visible where it belongs.
  • Board Settings is half-sourced. The button and its position come from Janover’s board [comp Aug 19]; the panel’s contents do not — we never saw inside theirs, so column visibility and card fields are conventional board settings [~]. Both act on real data: a hidden column’s deals stay in the pipeline and in every count (the note under the board says so), and each card field maps to a loan_requests column, named in the row’s hover.
  • Deal statuses are eight [💡 rec · 28 Aug] — Leads · Screening · Outreach · Quoting · Processing · Funded · Dead · On Hold, a collapse of Tony’s own eleven [T · Figma comment, 21 Aug]: Lead | Prospective Deal · Screening, Sizing & Packaging · Review Loan Request Package · Lender/Investor Outreach · Present Quotes to Client · Secure Term Sheet / Application · Engage Lender/Investor · Under App | Processing · Funded & Closed · Dead · On Hold, supplied verbatim in his comment on the Board view frame. The fold: the three packaging steps become Screening, quotes and term sheet become Quoting, lender engagement and underwriting become Processing. Dead and On Hold stay separate, as Tony had them: Dead is terminal and only feeds post-mortem reporting, On Hold is expected back — fusing them would bury recoverable deals among lost ones, the same trap as the retired fused Agency chip. His eleven had replaced the Janover placeholder set demoed 19 Aug. The schema fact stands: db/schema.rb confirms loan_requests has no status column at all, so this is a new column, not a rename.
  • Lender status and outreach are the real enums, from app/models/loan_request_match.rb and app/(dashboard)/lenders/types.ts: status is six values, disposition is five and its labels here are the repo's own DISPOSITION_OPTIONS strings. disposition: quoted is the product's Term Sheet.
  • Fit is computed, never typed. Five weighted criteria — amount 30, property type 25, geography 20, financing 15, appetite 10 — with property and geography as knock-outs and a financing miss capping below strong. The chips beside each score sum to it, so a 100% row shows five ticks. Drag the cut; every count on the page moves with it.
  • The deal summary is built only from columns that exist. Every field on it is a real loan_requests column — transaction_name, loan_amount_cents, property_type, transaction_summary, unit_quantity, physical_occupancy, sponsor_combined_net_worth and the rest. Nothing here needs a migration; the read layer is the whole gap.
  • Three controls on a deal row, three destinations — the name opens the deal, the match count opens the scored list, Find Lenders opens the same list with the cut released to 0 so the near-misses are visible, and scrolls straight to the Lenders on this deal section.
  • Observed vs sample. Four deal names and three of their locations are read off the shipped /loans index; Midtown Retail Permanent Debt is the only deal whose amount and property type we have seen ($3,100,000, Retail-Multi Tenant, Dallas TX), and Sandbox Debt Fund the only lender. A walk of the running product on 28 Aug moved three more values across that line: Riverside is Austin, Texas 78701 at $4,500,000 (not Houston at $12.0M), Northside’s financing type is Agency (Fannie Mae) on an industrial refinance, and Gulf Coast’s financing type is unset — the field is optional on the request form and that request left it blank, which is what the Not specified cell in the table now shows. Riverside’s unit count, purchase price, NOI and Sources & uses were re-scaled to the observed $4.5M loan so the summary cannot contradict its own header [~]; the numbers are still sample, only their magnitude is now anchored. Sponsors, summaries, the seven deals added for volume (Lakeline, Crescent, Pearl, Old Town, and — 26 Aug, for the board’s shape — Harborview, Magnolia, Foundry) and the other nineteen lenders are sample data, invented so the scoring and filters have something to bite on.
